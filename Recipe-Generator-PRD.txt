EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Recipe Generator is a no-sign-up cooking companion that helps people decide what to make using ingredients they already own. It combines guided ingredient input and meal preferences with a working sample recipe system, a searchable 100-recipe restaurant catalog, special dessert and drink menus, favorites, scalable servings, and a persistent shopping list.

Product promise:

From "what do I have?" to a cookable recipe in a few taps, without requiring an account or sending personal pantry data to a server.

The MVP uses reviewed sample recipes so the product remains useful before an AI service is connected. A dedicated generation function provides a clean boundary for adding an AI recipe-generation API later.


1. PRODUCT CONTEXT AND VISION

Problem

People frequently have food at home but lack a clear plan for turning it into a meal. Many recipe websites begin with recipe search instead of the user's actual pantry and can bury useful information behind sign-ins, long articles, or shopping-first workflows.

Vision

Make Recipe Generator the fastest and warmest way to turn a small list of available ingredients into appealing, actionable cooking ideas. The product should feel like a thoughtful neighborhood kitchen: inviting enough to inspire, structured enough to trust, and simple enough to use while standing at the counter.

Goals

- Let users enter ingredients one at a time, correct mistakes, and generate recipe ideas without an account.
- Provide at least three complete recipe suggestions with measurements, numbered steps, nutrition estimates, and missing-item guidance.
- Support the practical tasks that follow discovery: saving, copying, scaling servings, and building a categorized shopping list.
- Offer durable browsing through a searchable 100-recipe catalog and dedicated dessert and drink collections.
- Preserve favorites, shopping items, and settings locally across browser sessions.
- Create a clean integration boundary for an AI generation service without making the MVP dependent on one.
- Work well on phones, tablets, and desktop computers.

Non-goals for the MVP

- User accounts, social profiles, or cross-device cloud synchronization.
- Grocery ordering, delivery checkout, restaurant reservations, or payments.
- Medical-grade nutrition, allergy certification, or clinical dietary advice.
- Real-time pantry quantities, barcode scanning, or expiration tracking.
- Production AI generation. The initial release uses reviewed sample content.


2. USERS AND JOBS TO BE DONE

Primary users

The pantry-first home cook:
Has several ingredients but no meal plan and values speed, flexibility, and reduced food waste.

The busy household planner:
Needs time, difficulty, dietary, serving, and shopping controls in one place.

The inspiration browser:
Wants restaurant-style ideas, desserts, drinks, and visually appealing menus.

The newer cook:
Needs full measurements, numbered instructions, realistic timing, and clear difficulty labels.

Core jobs to be done

- When I do not know what to cook, help me use what I already have so I can avoid waste and decide quickly.
- When I have limited time or dietary needs, narrow the ideas so the result fits tonight's constraints.
- When a recipe looks good, give me everything needed to cook it without leaving the page.
- When I am missing ingredients, turn them into a useful shopping list.
- When I am browsing for inspiration, let me explore a broad menu and save ideas for later.

Primary journey

1. Add ingredients. Enter pantry items one at a time and review them as removable tags.
2. Tune preferences. Select meal type, available time, difficulty, dietary options, and exclusions.
3. Generate. Submit a valid pantry and see a loading state while ideas are prepared.
4. Choose. Compare at least three recipe suggestions and open one for complete details.
5. Act. Scale servings, save or copy the recipe, and add missing items to the shopping list.

4. INGREDIENT INPUT AND PREFERENCES

FR-01: Ingredient entry

The user can enter one ingredient at a time by selecting Add or pressing Enter. Leading and trailing whitespace is removed, and ingredient text is normalized to lowercase.

FR-02: Ingredient validation

The application rejects empty entries, entries without valid ingredient characters, and duplicates. A clear inline error explains how to recover.

FR-03: Ingredient tags

Every accepted ingredient appears as a removable tag. The remove button has an accessible label containing the ingredient name.

FR-04: Meal type

Supported values:

- Breakfast
- Lunch
- Dinner
- Snack
- Dessert

FR-05: Cooking time

Supported values:

- Under 15 minutes
- Under 30 minutes
- Under 1 hour
- Any time

FR-06: Difficulty

Supported values:

- Easy
- Medium
- Advanced

FR-07: Dietary options

The user may select multiple options:

- Vegetarian
- Vegan
- Gluten-free
- Dairy-free
- High-protein
- Low-carb

FR-08: Exclusions

The user can enter ingredients to exclude because of allergies or dislikes. Exclusions are collected separately from included pantry ingredients.

FR-09: Submission validation

The application must not generate recipes when the included ingredient list is empty. It must show an error that explains that at least one ingredient is required.


5. RECIPE GENERATION

FR-10: Loading state

While generating recipes, the application displays a warm cooking animation and short status message. Reduced-motion settings must minimize or disable decorative movement.

FR-11: Sample generation

The MVP returns five reviewed sample recipes so the product works without an AI API. A valid generation must return at least three complete suggestions.

FR-12: Regeneration

Generate Another Recipe repeats the generation flow without clearing pantry ingredients, preferences, or exclusions.

FR-13: Failure handling

If generation fails, the application shows a plain-language retry message. It must preserve all user input and any previously valid results.

Recipe suggestion cards include:

- Food image
- Recipe name
- Short description
- Total preparation and cooking time
- Difficulty
- Estimated calories
- Estimated protein
- Save control
- View full recipe action

The card grid uses three columns on wide screens, fewer columns on tablets, and one column on phones.


6. COMPLETE RECIPE DETAILS

Every recipe includes:

- Recipe name
- Food image
- Short description
- Preparation time
- Cooking time
- Difficulty level
- Original serving size
- Full ingredient list with numeric amounts and units
- Shopping category for every ingredient
- Numbered cooking instructions
- Estimated calories
- Estimated protein
- Clearly identified missing ingredients

Recipe tools

Save recipe:

- Toggle favorite state immediately.
- Persist the saved recipe ID locally.
- Show the recipe on the Saved Recipes page.
- Keep favorite state synchronized across cards, catalogs, and the detail dialog.

Copy or share:

- Copy the recipe name, scaled ingredient list, and numbered instructions to the clipboard.
- Confirm success or explain when the browser does not support copying.

Adjust servings:

- Increase or decrease servings.
- Do not allow fewer than one serving.
- Recalculate every ingredient amount using the ratio between current and original servings.

Add missing ingredients:

- Add only ingredients marked as missing.
- Prevent duplicate shopping items.
- Navigate to the Shopping List after adding items.

12. FUTURE AI INTEGRATION

The application exposes a dedicated asynchronous recipe-generation function. It accepts included ingredients and a structured preferences object and returns Recipe objects matching the product's required schema.

The MVP implementation returns reviewed sample recipes. A future provider integration must replace the implementation behind this function instead of coupling API logic directly to React components.

AI integration requirements

1. Validate before request. Require at least one included ingredient and sanitize all text fields.
2. Send structured constraints. Pass meal type, time, difficulty, dietary options, and exclusions separately.
3. Require schema-complete output. Reject or repair responses missing measurements, steps, timing, nutrition estimates, or missing-item flags.
4. Fail safely. Preserve user input and previous valid results when generation fails.
5. Protect secrets. Keep provider credentials on a server. Never embed API keys in client code or standalone HTML.
6. Retain fallback. Keep sample recipes available when the AI service is unavailable or not configured.


13. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Performance and reliability

- First meaningful content should appear quickly on typical mobile broadband.
- Large images should be responsive and efficiently served.
- Saving, filtering, serving adjustment, and shopping-list updates should feel immediate.
- The product remains usable when a food image fails to load.
- Generation failures do not erase ingredients, preferences, exclusions, favorites, settings, or shopping data.
- The standalone HTML file runs locally without a build step.
- The standalone HTML file does not require authentication.

Privacy and safety

- No account or identity data is required for MVP use.
- Pantry, preference, favorite, setting, and shopping data remain on the user's device in the sample version.
- Allergy exclusions are product constraints, not a guarantee of clinical safety.
- Nutrition values are estimates and are not medical advice.
- Any future AI provider must be disclosed before user data is transmitted.

17. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS

Risk: AI output may be incomplete or unsafe.
Severity: High.
Mitigation: Strict schema validation, exclusion prompts, disclaimers, and reviewed sample fallback.

Risk: Food image links may become unavailable.
Severity: Medium.
Mitigation: Use stable hosted assets, preserve text-first usability, and provide appropriate image alternatives.

Risk: Local storage is device-specific.
Severity: Medium.
Mitigation: Explain persistence clearly and consider optional export or accounts only after demand is demonstrated.

Risk: Nutrition estimates may appear authoritative.
Severity: Medium.
Mitigation: Label all values as estimates and avoid medical positioning.

Risk: A 100-item catalog may feel overwhelming.
Severity: Medium.
Mitigation: Provide prominent search, ten category filters, result counts, and clear empty-state recovery.

Risk: A large content surface can drift or become inconsistent.
Severity: Medium.
Mitigation: Use a shared Recipe schema, reusable components, automated validation, and editorial review.
